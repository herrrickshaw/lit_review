## What the deep-reads changed

The three papers converge on one finding: **the repo builds correlation matrices
and throws away the spectrum.** Verified, not assumed —
`grep -rniE 'eigen|effective_rank|absorption_ratio|marchenko'` over
`code/python_files/` (excluding vendored `.venv`) returns exactly one hit, and it
is a *comment* at `market_correlation_scan.py:306`. The matrix is built at
`:168` (full universe) and `:311` (MST path), consumed only as thresholded graph
edges at `:171-178`, then written to CSV. 2604.17251 supplies the upper-spectrum
formulas (absorption ratio, effective rank exp(H), spectral gap, MP excess) and
2608.09641 the lower-spectrum one (m⁻, eigenvalues below the lower MP bound) —
all from a single `eigvalsh` call on a matrix that already exists.

Second, independent find: `walk_forward_backtest.py:173-181` splits 60/20/20 with
a **zero-day gap** — test begins the trading day after train ends, while forward
returns span up to 8 horizons. ORCA matches its gap to its horizon; the repo
doesn't. This is a different leak site from queue item 6, which covers the
forward log.

Doing-right worth recording: the repo already models cost in its walk-forward
(`walk_forward_backtest.py:156`, 0.2% round-trip), which most of today's papers
don't, and `pe_bands.py:82-93` independently reached 2607.04958's
availability-vs-reference-time distinction.
