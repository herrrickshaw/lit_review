"Principal component error in high-dimensional factor models" (2026-09-17).**

Splits PC estimation error into an out-of-subspace term that is estimable from data
(`ℓ(p)/θⱼ(p)`, Theorem 2) and an in-subspace term that is not. Their setting is n growing with
T bounded; in their US-equity simulation the estimable half dominates.

Checked against the repo, and this is a direct hit:
`research/correlation_regime_spectrum.py` reads the market's correlation spectrum from a
**126-name matrix over a 60-day window** (`:34-35`, `:60`; `correlation_regime_spectrum.csv:2`
carries `n_symbols=126` on all 299 rows). **n/T ≈ 2.1** — rank ≤ 59, so at least 67 of the 126
eigenvalues are identically zero before any market structure is read off it. `spectral_stats()`
(`research/factor_redundancy.py:43-54`) applies no shrinkage and no null model; the only defence
is `np.clip(ev, 1e-12, None)` (`:44`). Queue item 9 asks for Marchenko–Pastur *descriptors* on a
different matrix; nothing asks whether the numbers already quoted from this script (queue lines
21-22) survive their own n/T.

Doing-right: it was built as a measurement script, not a gate (`:21`), so nothing live depends on
it; and it uses eigenvalues, not eigenvectors, which is far more robust in this regime.
